HOW I MODELED IT

How do I connect these tables?

01/Establish a rent bottom floor

I first sort out the number of apartments, area and unit rent by unit type, and then calculate the monthly rent and annual income. The development model calculates the single-family monthly rent as area × rent per square foot, which is then multiplied by the number of units.

Project Assumptions! D7 = E7 × C7; the income summary uses the rent of each apartment type × the number of units of each apartment type.
02/From revenue to NOI

I separate vacancy/credit losses and operating costs from revenue, which include items such as taxes, insurance, maintenance, and management. This can explain whether the change in NOI comes from the revenue side or the expense side.

RTL UW Model: B38 = B28 − B37; that is, NOI = Effective Gross Income − Operating Expenses.
03/Put costs into the timeline

I distinguish between land, hard costs, soft costs, contingency and financing charges. Construction Draw Schedule spreads the budget by type of work and month, rather than assuming that the construction money will be spent all at once.

Draw schedule uses the construction start date and EOMONTH generation date; Projected Cash Flows!F40 refers to the construction withdrawals of the corresponding month, and represents the expenditure as a negative number.
04 / Distinguish between asset cash flow and equity cash flow

I first calculate unlevered cash flow consisting of NOI, land, construction/soft costs and net sales proceeds, then add in acquisitions/refinancing, construction and long-term loan originations, fees, interest, principal and exit repayments.

Projected Cash Flows: row 48 is Unlevered Cash Flow; rows 61-78 are financing and debt services; row 81 is Levered Cash Flow.
05 / Find out the conditions that really limit loans

I calculate acquisition LTV, ARLTV, and LTC separately and convert each limit into an allowable loan amount. Loans cannot be decided solely on the most lenient basis.

UW Model!B67 = MIN(B64, B65, B66): Take the minimum value among the loan amounts corresponding to the three leverage constraints. B62 = B51 / B47; B63 = B51 / B20.
06 / Check debt services and interest reserves

I compare interest-only and amortized loans separately, checking the affordability of exit financing through NOI / annual debt service; construction carry is estimated using the average withdrawn balance.

B81 = B51 × B80 × B54 / 12; B82 = B81 × B55. The annual amortized debt service uses −PMT(monthly interest rate, total number of months, loan amount) × 12.
07/Use DCF and sensitivity to explain valuation

I used NOI and exit cap rate to derive the terminal value, then deducted the cost of sale and calculated the present value based on the discount rate. The DCF page displays both asset-level and equity-level results, as well as sensitivities to discount rate × exit cap rate and leverage × required equity return.

Core relationship: Terminal Value = next issue NOI / Exit Cap Rate after exit; Present Value = Cash Flow / (1 + Discount Rate)^number of issues. The document also contains the derivation of two discount rates, WACC and risk premium build-up.
08/From project income to LP/GP income

I connect fund terms to project cash flows, dealing with capital return, unpaid priority return, GP catch-up and residual promotion in order. After deducting the allocated cash at each level, you can proceed to the next level.

Waterfall!C47 = B47 + C45 − C46: Give priority to rolling back the arrears. C61 = MIN(C59, MAX(C60,0)): Pay the outstanding amount only with available cash. C185 = C181 − C182 − C183: Check the total allocation balance.

How to make a pricing engine?

First take MAX (SOFR, SOFR Floor) to get the effective base interest rate, then select spread according to product and experience level, and add leverage adjustment. All-in rate = Effective Base Rate + Adjusted Spread / 10,000. The output from the Pricing Engine needs to be reconciled with the loan rates, fees, and Term Sheet in the UW Model.

WORKBOOK 03 / WORK RECORD

Work record: After the model, what did I do?

This document captures the actual execution: updating underwriting budgets, comparing financing, preparing lender conditions, following up on drawdowns and closings, to insurance, vendor and payment processes.

BIDBUY Log / By Business

Organize actions, responsible persons, completion records and follow-up matters according to work and business categories.

Loan checklist

Track lender conditions, income/physical documents, insurance, title, loan and account information.

Record system configuration, supplier invitation, data collection, and supplier's corresponding project account.

Vendor ACH / Bills created

Record payment information, invoice, expiration, payment status, approver and accounting classification. Public pages do not display any account, supplier or invoice information.

RNR COI / Lending COI projects

Track insurance certificates, additional insured, certificates required for specific items, and coverage gaps.

Builders’ License / construction draw / FCI Account

Track license status, construction withdrawals, WIP, funding progress, loan accounts and payment follow-up.

audit / Closing Check / claims

Organize follow-up records of audit, delivery and claim materials. The subject of the claim, the lawsuit and the amount are not displayed.

How to feed work back to the model?

I use the updated construction budget to re-underwrite, supplement bad debt, concessions, rent losses and operating expenses, and then check the funding connection between construction loans, land refinancing and long-term loans. Changes in construction quotation, construction period and lease-up will affect cash flow and required equity; financing, withdrawal and delivery lists are responsible for implementing the capital plan in the model into data and execution.
